Quick Answer: How Do You Register in SAM.gov?

Start at the official SAM.gov entity registration page. Create or sign in with Login.gov, request or validate your Unique Entity ID, enter your legal business information, complete entity validation, add NAICS codes, banking and tax information, points of contact, and representations and certifications, then submit the registration for review.

SAM.gov is free to use. Be careful with third-party companies that make registration look like a paid requirement.

What Is SAM.gov?

SAM.gov is the federal System for Award Management. It is the main federal platform used for entity registration, Unique Entity ID, federal award visibility, assistance listings, contract opportunities, exclusions, responsibility data, and other federal procurement tools.

For contractors, SAM.gov matters because federal buyers, prime contractors, and procurement teams may ask for your UEI, CAGE code, active registration status, NAICS codes, representations and certifications, and points of contact.

Important: SAM.gov registration is a federal vendor setup step. It does not replace licensing, insurance, welding certifications, bonding, safety requirements, project specifications, engineering review, inspection requirements, state procurement registration, or buyer-specific onboarding.

SAM.gov Registration Checklist

The screens can change, but most contractors should prepare for the same basic registration path.

  1. Start on the official SAM.gov entity registration page. Use the official site and avoid old bookmarks, paid registration ads, or third-party forms.
  2. Create or access your Login.gov account. SAM.gov sign-in uses Login.gov. Make sure the person registering the entity has access to the correct business email and records.
  3. Request or validate your Unique Entity ID. The UEI identifies your entity in federal systems. If you only need a UEI and not full registration, SAM.gov has a separate path for that.
  4. Complete entity validation. Your legal business name and physical address need to match official records. Have documentation ready in case validation needs support.
  5. Enter business and tax information. Use the legal name, tax ID information, physical address, mailing address, and taxpayer information that matches your records.
  6. Add banking and payment details. Vendors registering for federal awards should enter accurate EFT/banking information for payment purposes.
  7. Select NAICS codes and business size information. Choose codes that match real services and buyer search behavior. For welding vendors, see the related Welding NAICS Code Guide.
  8. Complete representations and certifications. Review the questions carefully. These answers can affect eligibility, clauses, set-asides, and federal contracting records.
  9. Add points of contact. Keep government business, electronic business, past performance, accounts receivable, and other contacts current.
  10. Submit and monitor status. After submitting, check Entity Workspace and respond quickly if SAM.gov requests additional information.

Unique Entity ID, CAGE Code & SAM.gov Status

A Unique Entity ID is the federal identifier used in SAM.gov. A business can request a UEI alone or complete a full entity registration. Full registration is typically what vendors need when they want to directly receive federal contracts or assistance awards.

A CAGE code is used by the federal government for suppliers and is typically assigned or updated during the SAM.gov entity registration process for U.S. entities. Once issued, keep your SAM.gov profile accurate so primes and federal buyers have the correct information.

Choosing NAICS Codes in SAM.gov

SAM.gov asks vendors to select NAICS codes that describe the business. For a welding contractor, the right codes depend on whether the work is repair, fabrication, structural steel, field installation, bridge welding, facility maintenance, or commercial construction support.

Common SAM.gov Registration Mistakes

  • Using a business name or address that does not match official records
  • Assuming a UEI alone is the same as full active registration
  • Letting the registration expire before a bid or award
  • Choosing NAICS codes that do not match real services
  • Using outdated or inactive points of contact
  • Skipping review of representations and certifications
  • Ignoring validation or follow-up requests after submission
  • Paying unnecessary third parties because they made SAM.gov look like a paid service

SAM.gov vs. eVA vs. SWaM

SAM.gov, eVA, and SWaM are connected to procurement, but they are not the same thing.

System What It Is Why It Matters
SAM.gov Federal entity registration and federal procurement platform Used for UEI, CAGE, federal award eligibility, reps and certs, and federal vendor visibility.
Virginia eVA Virginia electronic procurement marketplace Used by Virginia public-sector buyers and suppliers for state, university, college, and local government opportunities.
SWaM Virginia Small, Women-owned, and Minority-owned Business certification Shows certified small business status for Virginia procurement opportunities when applicable.

SAM.gov Registration FAQ

Is SAM.gov registration free?

Yes. SAM.gov states there is no cost to use SAM.gov. Contractors should use the official website and be cautious of paid third-party services that imply payment is required.

Do I need a UEI before registering?

The Unique Entity ID is part of SAM.gov. You can request a UEI or complete full entity registration depending on what you need.

Is a UEI the same as active SAM.gov registration?

No. A UEI identifies the entity, but a full active registration includes additional business, tax, banking, NAICS, points of contact, and representations/certifications information.

How long does SAM.gov registration take?

Timing can vary, especially if entity validation or supporting documentation is needed. Start before you need to bid and monitor Entity Workspace for follow-up requests.

Do subcontractors need SAM.gov?

Some subcontractors may be asked for UEI, CAGE, SAM.gov status, or capability information by primes or agencies. Direct federal awardees generally need active SAM.gov registration.

How does SAM.gov relate to a welding capability statement?

Your SAM.gov registration should match your capability statement, NAICS codes, certifications, past performance, service pages, and buyer-facing procurement information.
