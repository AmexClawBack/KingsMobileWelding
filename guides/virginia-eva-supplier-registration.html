How to Register as a Supplier in Virginia eVA

Quick Answer: How Do You Register as a Supplier in Virginia eVA?

Start on the official eVA registration page, create or access the supplier registration portal, enter your legal business information, add contacts and addresses, choose commodity codes, complete your profile, upload required documents, and submit the Commonwealth of Virginia Substitute W-9 according to the current eVA instructions.

eVA is useful because it helps suppliers become visible to Virginia State Agencies, Colleges and Universities, and many Local Governments that use eVA to post opportunities, get quotes, and place orders.

What Is Virginia eVA?

eVA is Virginia’s electronic procurement marketplace. For suppliers, contractors, and service providers, eVA is one of the main systems used to find and receive public-sector opportunity notifications in Virginia.

Registered suppliers may be placed on bidder lists and receive opportunity notifications based on the profile information, commodity codes, and notification settings selected during registration.

Important: eVA registration is a procurement visibility step. It does not replace licensing, insurance, certifications, safety requirements, bonding, engineering review, project specifications, SWaM certification, SAM.gov registration, or buyer-specific onboarding.

Step-by-Step eVA Supplier Registration Checklist

The exact eVA screens can change, but the process usually follows the same basic path. Use this checklist to prepare before you start.

  1. Start on the official eVA registration page. Use the official eVA “Register Now” supplier registration path. Avoid old bookmarks or outdated third-party instructions when you are ready to actually register.
  2. Create or access the supplier account. Set up the organization or supplier profile and verify the email/user information required by the portal.
  3. Enter legal business information. Use the legal business name, tax information, address information, and contact details that match your business records.
  4. Add contacts and addresses. Add the correct order, physical, billing, payment, and primary contact information as prompted. Make sure the right person receives bid notices and supplier emails.
  5. Select commodity codes. Choose commodity codes that match your real services, not just broad categories. Commodity codes affect how buyers and bid notifications may find your business.
  6. Upload required documents. Follow the current eVA instructions for the Commonwealth of Virginia Substitute W-9 and any other requested supplier documents.
  7. Review profile status and notifications. Confirm that your supplier profile is complete, accurate, and set up to receive the correct notifications.
  8. Keep the profile updated. Update contacts, addresses, commodity codes, insurance documents, certifications, and capabilities when your business changes.

Common eVA Registration Mistakes

Supplier registration is not only about filling out forms. Your profile should help buyers understand what you actually do and how to contact you.

  • Choosing commodity codes that are too broad or not related to actual services
  • Leaving out important service keywords in the business description
  • Using an inactive email address for bid notifications
  • Forgetting to upload or complete the Commonwealth of Virginia Substitute W-9
  • Not updating contact information after staff changes
  • Listing services without matching documentation, insurance, or past performance
  • Assuming eVA registration is the same as SWaM certification or SAM.gov registration

eVA vs. SWaM Certification

eVA registration and SWaM certification are related to Virginia procurement, but they are not the same thing.

eVA is the marketplace and procurement portal. SWaM certification is Virginia’s certification program for eligible Small, Women-owned, Minority-owned, Micro, and related supplier categories administered through the Virginia Department of Small Business and Supplier Diversity.

A business may need both: eVA registration to participate in procurement activity and SWaM certification to show certified small business status when applicable.

Tip for small businesses: If your company qualifies for SWaM, keep your eVA profile, SWaM profile, commodity codes, capability statement, and website service pages aligned. Buyers should see the same service capabilities in every place they check.

eVA vs. SAM.gov

eVA is Virginia’s procurement marketplace. SAM.gov is the federal system used for federal registration and federal contracting visibility.

A contractor that wants to support both Virginia public-sector buyers and federal or prime contractor work may keep eVA, SWaM, SAM.gov, capability statements, NAICS codes, safety documents, and past performance updated together.

Virginia eVA Supplier Registration FAQ

Is eVA supplier registration free?

The official eVA supplier information page states that eVA registration is free to join and gives registered suppliers access to tools such as bidder list placement and opportunity notifications.

Do I need a Commonwealth of Virginia Substitute W-9?

The official eVA registration page states that supplier registration is not considered complete unless a Commonwealth of Virginia Substitute W-9 is received. Follow the current eVA instructions for signing, dating, and uploading the form.

How do I choose the right eVA commodity codes?

Choose codes that match the work you actually perform and how buyers search. A welding contractor may need codes related to welding, metal fabrication, structural steel, repair, facility maintenance, emergency repair, industrial repair, or construction support depending on the services offered.

Is eVA the same as SWaM certification?

No. eVA is the procurement marketplace and supplier registration system. SWaM certification is a separate Virginia small business certification program administered through the Virginia Department of Small Business and Supplier Diversity.

Is eVA the same as SAM.gov?

No. eVA is for Virginia procurement activity. SAM.gov is used for federal registration and federal contracting. Businesses pursuing both Virginia and federal work may need both profiles, depending on their goals.

What should a vendor profile include besides registration?

A strong vendor setup should include accurate contact information, commodity codes, capability descriptions, service pages, certification information, insurance documentation when requested, past performance, and a clear process for receiving opportunity notices.
