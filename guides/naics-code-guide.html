Quick Answer: What Is the NAICS Code for Welding?

There is not one single NAICS code that fits every welding business. For many welding repair and mobile welding service companies, NAICS 811310 is often the closest direct match because the official description includes welding repair services. For structural steel installation, 238120 may apply. For structural metal fabrication, 332312 may apply. For ornamental or architectural metal work like railings, stairs, and similar metal products, 332323 may apply.

The best code depends on your primary activity: repair, fabrication, structural steel, construction installation, facility maintenance, or manufacturing.

What Is a NAICS Code?

NAICS stands for the North American Industry Classification System. It is used to classify businesses by industry so agencies, buyers, data systems, procurement portals, and contracting teams can organize companies and opportunities consistently.

For welding vendors, NAICS codes matter because buyers may search for vendors by code, solicitations may be assigned a code, and procurement profiles often ask suppliers to select the codes that match their real work.

Important: NAICS codes are classification and procurement search tools. They do not replace licensing, insurance, welding certifications, OSHA/safety requirements, engineering review, inspection requirements, or project specifications.

Why 811310 Often Shows Up for Welding Repair

Many people search for “welding NAICS code” because they need a code for a business registration, SAM.gov profile, state procurement portal, insurance form, tax form, or customer vendor setup package.

For a mobile welding business that primarily performs repair and maintenance work, 811310 is often relevant because it covers commercial and industrial machinery and equipment repair and maintenance and includes welding repair services in the official NAICS description.

Examples of welding work that may point toward a repair/service code include:

  • Emergency mobile welding repairs
  • Equipment repair welding
  • Industrial plant repair welding
  • Broken brackets, cracked steel, guards, supports, and maintenance welding
  • Commercial facility welding repairs
  • Field repair work that is not primarily manufacturing a new product

When 238120 Fits Structural Steel Welding

If the work is tied to structural steel installation, steel erection, field-welded connections, beams, columns, steel construction products, or construction-phase structural work, 238120 may be a better fit than a general welding repair code.

This can matter for contractors bidding commercial construction, structural steel scopes, public works, industrial upgrades, building steel, and contractor-directed field welding.

  • AWS D1.1 structural steel welding
  • Beam and column welding
  • Field-welded structural steel connections
  • Embed plate, base plate, and anchor-related steel work
  • Steel joist repair and reinforcement
  • Commercial construction field welding

Related pages: structural steel welding, field-welded structural steel connections and splices, beam and column repair welding, embed plate, base plate, and anchor rod welding, and steel joist repair and reinforcement welding.

When 332312 Fits Structural Metal Fabrication

If the business is primarily fabricating structural metal products rather than only installing or repairing them in the field, 332312 may be relevant. This can apply when the main activity is producing structural steel assemblies, fabricated structural components, rebar assemblies, bar joists, or similar structural metal products.

For a welding contractor that does both field welding and fabrication, 332312 may be a secondary or supporting code, depending on actual work mix and how the company is registered.

When 332323 Fits Railings, Stairs & Architectural Metal

332323 is commonly relevant when the primary activity is ornamental and architectural metal work manufacturing. For welding vendors, this may apply to shops or contractors fabricating railings, stairs, fire escapes, open steel flooring, scaffolding, and similar architectural metal products.

Related work may include commercial welding, fixed ladder installation, bollard installation, railing repairs, stair repairs, access platforms, and other commercial metalwork.

NAICS Codes for Government Contracting, SAM.gov & Procurement

In government contracting and public-sector procurement, NAICS codes are often used to categorize opportunities, assign size standards, search vendor profiles, and organize solicitations. A solicitation usually has a NAICS code assigned by the buyer or contracting office.

For a welding vendor, the practical goal is not to add every possible metal-related code. The better goal is to list codes that match real services, past performance, and the kind of work buyers actually search for.

Procurement tip: Use NAICS codes together with keywords, past performance, capability statements, service pages, and commodity codes. Buyers often search by more than one signal.

NAICS vs. NIGP Codes

NAICS codes classify industries. NIGP codes classify commodities and services for procurement. In Virginia and local government purchasing, NIGP or commodity codes may be just as important as NAICS codes because buyers may search for “welding,” “building maintenance,” “bridge construction,” “facility repair,” or similar categories through purchasing systems.

For welding vendors working through public purchasing portals, it is usually smart to keep both NAICS and NIGP/commodity codes aligned with the real scope.

How to Choose the Best NAICS Code for a Welding Business

Start with the primary activity, not the broadest possible code. Ask what the business does most often and what the customer is actually buying.

  1. Identify the main revenue activity. Is the business mostly repair, fabrication, construction installation, facility maintenance, or manufacturing?
  2. Match the code to real work. Do not list codes just because they sound close if they do not match actual scopes.
  3. Think like a buyer. A facility manager may search repair and maintenance. A GC may search structural steel. A procurement officer may search by commodity and service keywords.
  4. Use multiple codes carefully. Multiple codes can make sense when the business performs multiple real services.
  5. Check the official source. Use the current Census NAICS lookup, SBA size standards, and the buyer’s solicitation instructions when the code matters for contracting.

Common Mistakes Welding Vendors Make With NAICS Codes

  • Using only a manufacturing code when the business is mainly repair/service work
  • Using only a repair code when the scope is really structural steel construction work
  • Adding too many unrelated codes to a procurement profile
  • Forgetting local commodity/NIGP codes in state and municipal purchasing systems
  • Assuming a NAICS code replaces licensing, insurance, certification, or engineering requirements
  • Failing to connect NAICS codes with service pages, capability statements, and past performance

Welding NAICS Code FAQ

What is the best NAICS code for welding?

There is no single best code for every welding business. Many welding repair businesses may fit 811310, structural steel contractor work may fit 238120, structural metal fabrication may fit 332312, and ornamental or architectural metal fabrication may fit 332323.

Is 811310 the NAICS code for welding?

811310 is commonly relevant for welding repair and mobile welding repair services because the official NAICS description includes welding repair services. It may not fit every welding business, especially if the company is primarily manufacturing or performing structural steel construction work.

What NAICS code should I use for mobile welding?

Mobile welding often falls under 811310 when the work is repair and maintenance. If the mobile welding work is structural steel installation, construction, fabrication, bridge welding, or facility support, another code may also be relevant.

What NAICS code applies to structural steel welding?

238120 is commonly relevant for structural steel contractor work, while 332312 may apply when the primary activity is fabricating structural metal products. Project specifications and buyer instructions matter.

Can I list multiple NAICS codes?

Yes. A vendor profile can reference multiple NAICS codes when those codes match actual services and past work. The best approach is to use codes that reflect real scopes buyers would reasonably search for.

Do NAICS codes determine small business size standards?

SBA size standards are tied to individual NAICS codes for federal contracting. Contractors should check the current SBA size standards table when size status matters for a solicitation or set-aside opportunity.

Are NAICS codes the same as NIGP codes?

No. NAICS codes classify industries. NIGP and commodity codes classify goods and services used in purchasing systems. Public buyers may use both when searching for welding vendors.
