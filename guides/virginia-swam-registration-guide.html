Virginia SWaM Registration Guide: Small, Women-Owned & Minority-Owned Business Certification

Quick Answer: How Do You Apply for Virginia SWaM Certification?

Start with the Virginia Department of Small Business and Supplier Diversity certification information, confirm which certification type fits your business, gather ownership, control, tax, business, and supporting documentation, complete the online certification application, respond to any SBSD requests, and keep your certification information aligned with your eVA supplier profile and capability statement.

SWaM certification is separate from eVA supplier registration. A strong Virginia procurement setup often includes both.

What Is SWaM Certification?

SWaM stands for Small, Women-owned, and Minority-owned Business. In Virginia, SWaM certification is administered by the Department of Small Business and Supplier Diversity and helps eligible businesses qualify for specialized procurement and contracting opportunities.

For contractors, suppliers, and service providers, SWaM certification can help public buyers identify certified small businesses, micro businesses, women-owned businesses, minority-owned businesses, service-disabled veteran-owned businesses, and related certified supplier categories in Virginia procurement systems.

Important: SWaM certification is not the same as licensing, insurance, OSHA/safety compliance, welding certification, bonding, engineering review, or project qualification. Certification helps with procurement identity, but buyers still review capability, pricing, documents, safety, experience, and scope fit.

SWaM Registration Checklist

The specific questions and documents can vary by business structure and certification type, but most applicants should prepare the same basic categories of information.

  1. Confirm the correct certification type. Review SBSD certification information and decide whether you are applying as Small, Micro, Women-owned, Minority-owned, Service Disabled Veteran-owned, or another eligible category.
  2. Create or access the certification portal. Use the official SBSD certification application portal and keep login information with the person responsible for certification updates.
  3. Gather ownership and control documents. Be ready to show who owns, manages, controls, and operates the business.
  4. Gather business and tax records. Have formation documents, tax information, operating agreements, licenses, resumes, bank signatures, and other records ready when applicable.
  5. Describe real services clearly. Explain what your company actually does, who you serve, where you work, and what procurement buyers should contact you for.
  6. Submit the application and monitor requests. Respond quickly if SBSD asks for clarification, additional documents, signatures, or corrections.
  7. Align SWaM with eVA. Make sure your eVA supplier profile, commodity codes, service descriptions, website, and capability statement match your certified business profile.
  8. Maintain certification information. Track expiration, recertification, ownership changes, contact changes, address changes, and business status updates.

What Documents Should You Prepare?

Document requirements can depend on the business structure, ownership category, certification type, and SBSD review. Use the official SBSD instructions as the final checklist.

  • Formation documents

  • Operating agreement or bylaws if applicable
  • Business address and contact information
  • Business licenses or registrations if applicable
  • Management/control information

Procurement & Capability Information

  • eVA supplier profile
  • NAICS and commodity codes
  • Capability statement
  • Past performance examples
  • Website service pages
  • Insurance and safety information when requested
  • Certification and compliance information

Micro Business Designation in Virginia

Virginia’s Micro Business designation can be important for very small companies. SBSD describes Micro Business as a designation for businesses with no more than 25 employees and no more than $3 million in average annual revenue over the three-year period before certification.

For small contractors, this may be one of the most useful designations to understand because some procurement opportunities and buyer goals may specifically reference small or micro businesses.

SWaM vs. eVA vs. SAM.gov

SWaM, eVA, and SAM.gov are all tied to procurement visibility, but they serve different purposes.

System / Program What It Is Why It Matters
SWaM Certification Virginia small, women-owned, minority-owned, micro, and related business certification program Helps buyers identify certified suppliers and supports Virginia procurement participation.
Virginia eVA Virginia procurement marketplace and supplier registration system Helps suppliers receive bid notifications and become visible to Virginia public-sector buyers.
SAM.gov Federal entity registration and federal procurement platform Used for UEI, CAGE, federal awards, reps and certs, and federal vendor registration.

How SWaM Helps Contractors and Service Businesses

SWaM certification does not guarantee work, but it helps buyers identify certified suppliers. It can support outreach, set-aside opportunities, supplier searches, subcontracting goals, agency reporting, and procurement visibility.

The certification is strongest when it is backed by a clear capability statement, active eVA profile, strong service pages, clear past performance, responsive estimating, and professional documentation.

Common SWaM Application Mistakes

  • Starting the application before gathering ownership/control records
  • Using unclear business descriptions that do not match actual services
  • Failing to align eVA commodity codes with the company’s real capabilities
  • Not responding quickly to SBSD document or clarification requests
  • Assuming SWaM certification is the same as eVA registration
  • Assuming SWaM certification replaces insurance, licensing, safety, or welding qualifications
  • Forgetting to update business contact information after staff or email changes
  • Letting certification information get out of sync with the website, capability statement, and procurement profiles

Virginia SWaM Registration FAQ

What does SWaM stand for?

SWaM stands for Small, Women-owned, and Minority-owned Business. Virginia also recognizes related designations such as Micro Business and Service Disabled Veteran-owned status through state supplier diversity programs.

Who administers SWaM certification?

The Virginia Department of Small Business and Supplier Diversity administers SWaM certification and other supplier diversity certification programs.

How long is SWaM certification valid?

Virginia regulations state that initial certification is for five years unless the certification is revoked before that period, the business is no longer in business, or the business is no longer eligible.

Is SWaM the same as eVA?

No. eVA is Virginia’s procurement marketplace and supplier registration system. SWaM is a certification program. A business may need both to improve procurement visibility.

Is SWaM the same as SAM.gov?

No. SAM.gov is the federal entity registration system. SWaM is a Virginia certification program. Contractors pursuing both Virginia and federal work may keep both profiles current.

What should a welding contractor include in a SWaM profile?

A welding contractor should describe actual capabilities such as mobile welding, structural steel welding, bridge welding support, commercial welding, facility maintenance welding, emergency repair, fabrication, past performance, and service area.
