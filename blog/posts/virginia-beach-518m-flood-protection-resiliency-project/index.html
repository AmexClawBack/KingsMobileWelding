Coastal cities are investing heavily in flood protection, stormwater improvements and resiliency infrastructure. A public example in Hampton Roads is the Windsor Woods, Princess Anne Plaza and The Lakes Stormwater Improvements project in Virginia Beach, a $518 million progressive design-build project that has moved into construction after a collaborative preconstruction phase with the City of Virginia Beach, FlatironDragados and Arcadis.

Virginia Beach’s $518M Flood Protection Project: What Is Being Built?

The Windsor Woods, Princess Anne Plaza and The Lakes Stormwater Improvements project is designed to improve stormwater management and reduce flooding in neighborhoods that have historically experienced severe flooding. Public project descriptions identify major elements such as large pump stations, tide gates to minimize tidal impacts, stormwater storage, flood barriers and drainage channel upgrades.

These are not simple drainage repairs. They are large, interconnected systems that have to manage rainfall, tidal influence, downstream water levels, creek conditions, neighborhood impacts, access, utility work, environmental permitting and construction staging. For contractors, that means the work depends on more than earthwork and concrete. It also creates demand for structural steel, access steel, embedded items, support frames, temporary works, cofferdams, sheet pile and field modifications.

Why Tide Gates and Pump Stations Need Heavy Civil Coordination

Tide gates and pump stations are designed to help control how water enters, exits and stores within the drainage system. Public City of Virginia Beach materials explain that the Windsor Woods Pump Station will work with the Windsor Woods Tide Gate to lower water levels before extreme tidal events and major forecasted storms, creating stormwater storage capacity and helping move stormwater during major rainfall.

That kind of water-control system requires coordination between civil construction, structural components, electrical/mechanical systems, concrete, access roads, gates, pump station structures, inspection, environmental controls and field installation. Welding support may be needed around support steel, frames, embedded plates, railing and access components, cofferdam steel, temporary works, field repairs and contractor-directed modifications.

Where Field Welding Shows Up on Flood Protection Projects

Field welding on flood protection work is usually tied to a specific construction need. It may be planned in the drawings, requested because of field conditions, needed for a temporary works phase, required to close an inspection item or used to support access and installation around a pump station, tide gate or flood barrier.

  • Sheet pile and cofferdams: walers, struts, braces, tie-ins, closure plates, cap plates and contractor-directed temporary works.
  • Pump station support steel: frames, platforms, brackets, access steel, equipment support steel and structural attachments when specified.
  • Tide gate and flood barrier support: structural steel, embedded plates, connection steel, access components and field modifications.
  • Bridge and DOT interfaces: bridge-adjacent steel, remediation details, support steel, inspection repairs and AWS D1.5 bridge welding support when required by the project.
  • Heavy civil modifications: repair plates, stiffeners, brackets, field fit-up corrections, punch-list welding and schedule-driven support.

Cofferdams, Sheet Pile and Temporary Works

A key welding-related takeaway from flood protection and tide gate construction is the importance of temporary works. Public U.S. Army Corps of Engineers documentation for the related The Lakes and Princess Anne Plaza drainage improvements describes construction of tide gates, weir gates, wing wall areas, pump station grading/filling, maintenance and access roads, stormwater utilities and dredging. The same notice explains that constructing a tide gate in two cofferdam sections was proposed to avoid additional impacts and residential property acquisition compared with a diversion-channel alternative.

That is exactly the type of setting where sheet pile welding and cofferdam welding become important to contractors. Cofferdams may require sheet pile walls, walers, struts, bracing, tie-ins, closure plates, support steel, temporary access steel and field modifications. The welding must fit the excavation sequence, dewatering plan, inspection hold points and the contractor’s production schedule.

Bridge, DOT and Infrastructure Welding Requirements

Flood protection projects often touch bridge, roadway, drainage, utility and public infrastructure. The welding requirements depend on the contract documents. AWS D1.1 structural welding may apply to many structural steel scopes. AASHTO/AWS D1.5 applies to welded highway bridges and bridge components, including shop and field fabrication of steel bridges and bridge components. When an infrastructure project includes bridge-related welding, the responsible engineer, owner, DOT requirements and project specifications should determine whether AWS D1.5 is required.

Kings Mobile Welding supports AWS D1.1 structural welding and AWS D1.5 bridge welding support when specified. The important point for contractors is to identify welding requirements early: code, WPS, material, inspection, documentation, CWI/NDT needs, access, hot work requirements and schedule.

Why Progressive Design-Build Projects Need Field-Ready Subcontractors

The City has described the Mega Bundle as moving forward with a progressive design-build contract, which allows design and construction activities to overlap so work can start sooner and move faster. On projects like that, constructability and field coordination matter from the start.

Field-ready welding subcontractors can help contractors plan realistic welding access, field fit-up, material handling, temporary works support, repair welding, punch-list support and project-specific mobilization. The more complex the jobsite, the more important it is to understand actual field conditions instead of assuming every detail will fit perfectly.

Critical Infrastructure Welding FAQ

Where does field welding show up on flood protection projects?

Field welding can show up on sheet pile cofferdams, walers, bracing, access steel, pump station support steel, embedded plates, structural frames, tide gate support components, platforms, railings, repair plates, field modifications and inspection-driven heavy civil welding items.

Do flood protection projects use sheet pile and cofferdams?

Many flood protection, tide gate, pump station and bridge-related scopes use sheet pile, cofferdams, temporary works, excavation support, walers, struts and bracing to build or access the work area safely and control water or soil during construction.

Does Kings Mobile Welding provide engineering for flood protection or tide gate work?

No. Kings Mobile Welding does not provide engineering or act as the engineer of record. The responsible engineer, project specifications, owner, inspector, DOT authority or qualified authority determines design, member sizes, weld details, inspection requirements and code requirements.

Can Kings Mobile Welding support critical infrastructure welding scopes?

Yes. Kings Mobile Welding can support contractor-directed field welding scopes for structural steel, sheet pile, cofferdams, heavy civil work, bridge/DOT work, industrial infrastructure, pump station support steel, access steel and project-specific field modifications when drawings and project requirements are provided.

Does AWS D1.5 apply to all infrastructure welding?

No. AASHTO/AWS D1.5 applies to welded highway bridges and bridge components. For flood protection or stormwater infrastructure, the applicable welding code depends on the contract documents, owner requirements, project specifications and the responsible engineer’s direction.
