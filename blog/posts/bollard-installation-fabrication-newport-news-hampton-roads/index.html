What Are Bollards Used For?

Bollards are vertical posts used to protect, separate, guide, or restrict access. In commercial and industrial settings, they are often installed around buildings, equipment, doors, sidewalks, parking areas, loading docks, utilities, and facility traffic zones.

Common uses include:

  • Protecting storefront glass, entrances, building corners, and pedestrian areas
  • Protecting overhead doors, roll-up doors, dock doors, and warehouse openings
  • Separating vehicles from mechanical equipment, electrical cabinets, and utilities
  • Creating controlled access points with chain bollards or gate bollards
  • Protecting equipment from forklifts, delivery trucks, trailers, carts, and site traffic
  • Guiding vehicles through parking lots, pickup lanes, service lanes, and loading areas
  • Supporting commercial property protection upgrades after repeated damage

Bollard Installation vs. Bollard Fabrication

Bollard installation and bollard fabrication are related, but they are not the same. Installation is the field work of setting, mounting, anchoring, drilling, welding, or securing the bollards at the site. Fabrication is the shop or field preparation of the bollards themselves.

Fabrication may include cutting steel pipe or tube, welding caps, adding welded eyelets, drilling plates, building base plates, preparing chain attachment points, painting, or coordinating powder coat.

Installation may include layout, field measurements, drilling, anchoring, welding, setting bollards, coordinating concrete, confirming spacing, and completing the work around active site conditions.

The best projects plan both together. That way, the bollard size, spacing, attachment, finish, and installation method match the site.

Types of Steel Bollards for Commercial Properties

The right bollard depends on the location, traffic exposure, surface, owner requirements, and what is being protected. Some sites only need basic steel pipe bollards. Others need custom bollards with chain attachment points, base plates, sleeves, or a specific finish.

Steel Pipe Bollards

Steel pipe bollards are common for parking lots, storefronts, warehouses, service lanes, gates, loading areas, and general property protection. They are often painted safety yellow so drivers, forklift operators, and delivery crews can see them clearly.

Surface-Mounted Bollards

Surface-mounted bollards are typically attached to concrete with a base plate and anchors. This type of installation may be used when a bolt-down detail is preferred or when the owner does not want an embedded bollard.

Embedded or Core-Drilled Bollards

Embedded bollards may be set into concrete, core-drilled locations, or owner-directed foundation details. The concrete thickness, slab condition, rebar, edge distance, drainage, and underground utilities should be confirmed before drilling or setting bollards.

Custom Chain Bollards

Custom chain bollards can be fabricated with welded eyelets, chain hooks, tabs, loops, or other access-control points. These are commonly used to block service lanes, private drive areas, restricted zones, or temporary access openings.

Equipment Protection Bollards

Equipment protection bollards are used around HVAC units, electrical panels, meters, generators, dock equipment, gates, warehouse machinery, facility assets, and other equipment that may be exposed to vehicles or forklifts.

How Commercial Bollard Installation Is Planned

A good bollard installation starts with understanding the site. The same bollard detail will not work for every building, slab, warehouse, storefront, or parking lot.

Before installation, the contractor or property team should consider:

  • What the bollards are protecting
  • How many bollards are needed
  • Desired spacing between bollards
  • Height above finished grade
  • Steel diameter and wall thickness
  • Whether the bollards will be surface-mounted, embedded, core-drilled, removable, or chain-style
  • Concrete condition, slab thickness, drainage, and nearby edges
  • Traffic flow, forklift routes, vehicle turning radius, and pedestrian paths
  • Finish requirements such as safety yellow paint, powder coat, galvanized, or owner-selected color
Site planning tip: Send both close-up photos and wide-angle photos. Close-ups show the concrete, anchors, and bollard locations. Wide photos show traffic flow, access, equipment, doors, sidewalks, and what the bollards need to protect.

Surface-Mounted vs. Embedded Bollards

One of the first decisions is whether the bollards will be surface-mounted or embedded. Surface-mounted bollards usually use a base plate and anchors into concrete. Embedded bollards may be set into concrete, installed in core-drilled holes, or coordinated with a concrete contractor.

Surface-mounted bollards can be helpful when the owner wants a bolt-down detail or when the bollard location needs to stay above the existing slab. Embedded bollards may be preferred for certain protective layouts, but the site conditions matter.

Concrete thickness, reinforcement, underground utilities, edge distance, drainage, slab condition, and engineered requirements should be confirmed before installation. When bollards are expected to resist vehicle impact or meet a rated security requirement, the system should be specified by the responsible engineer, manufacturer, owner, project documents, or qualified authority.

Can Bollards Be Painted or Powder Coated Safety Yellow?

Yes. Safety yellow is a common finish for bollards because it improves visibility around vehicles, forklifts, loading areas, doors, and equipment. Depending on the project, finish options may include field paint, shop paint, powder coat, galvanized finish, or an owner-selected color.

If you need a specific coating system, safety color, powder coat finish, or owner-required paint, include that requirement before fabrication starts. Finish requirements can affect pricing, schedule, and installation timing.

Are Bollards Crash-Rated?

Standard steel pipe bollards are not automatically crash-rated. A bollard is only crash-rated, anti-ram rated, or vehicle impact-rated when the bollard system, foundation, installation detail, manufacturer requirements, testing, and documentation support that rating.

For storefront protection, warehouse protection, general property protection, or equipment protection, many owners use standard protective bollards. For security-rated applications, public safety requirements, high-risk vehicle impact areas, or engineered protection, the detail should be specified by the responsible engineer, owner, manufacturer, project documents, security consultant, or qualified authority.

Important: Do not assume a field-fabricated bollard is crash-rated. Crash-rated bollard systems require the correct rated product, foundation, installation method, and documentation.
