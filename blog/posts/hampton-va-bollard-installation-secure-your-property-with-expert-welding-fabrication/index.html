Bollard Installation vs. Bollard Fabrication

Bollard installation is the field work: layout, drilling, anchoring, setting, welding, or securing the bollards at the property. Bollard fabrication is the preparation of the bollards themselves: cutting steel pipe or tube, welding caps, adding eyelets or chain tabs, fabricating base plates, preparing sleeves, and finishing the steel.

For many Hampton Roads properties, the best solution is both: fabricate the bollards to fit the site, then install them correctly around the actual building, concrete, traffic pattern, and access restrictions.

Types of Bollards We Can Install or Fabricate

Different sites need different bollard details. A retail storefront may need visible safety-yellow pipe bollards. A warehouse may need heavy-use bollards around roll-up doors and forklift lanes. A property manager may need custom chain bollards to block off a lane or restricted area.

Steel Pipe Bollards

Steel pipe bollards are a common choice for storefronts, parking lots, warehouses, gates, loading areas, and general commercial property protection. They are often painted or powder coated safety yellow for visibility.

Surface-Mounted Bollards

Surface-mounted bollards typically use a base plate and anchors into an existing concrete slab. They may be useful when a bolt-down detail is preferred or when the owner does not want the bollard embedded into concrete.

Embedded or Core-Drilled Bollards

Embedded bollards may be set into concrete, core-drilled locations, or owner-directed foundation details. Concrete condition, slab thickness, rebar, edge distance, drainage, and underground utilities should be reviewed before drilling or setting bollards.

Custom Chain Bollards

Custom chain bollards can be fabricated with welded eyelets, chain loops, hooks, tabs, or other access-control points. They are useful for blocking off lanes, private entrances, service areas, temporary openings, or restricted zones.

Equipment Protection Bollards

Equipment protection bollards are used around HVAC units, generators, electrical gear, mechanical units, utility meters, gates, dock equipment, warehouse machinery, and other assets that could be hit by vehicles or forklifts.

What to Consider Before Installing Bollards

A good bollard installation starts with understanding the site. The same bollard detail will not work for every storefront, warehouse, parking lot, or industrial facility.

Before installation, the property team should consider:

  • What the bollards are protecting
  • How many bollards are needed
  • Desired spacing and layout
  • Height above finished grade
  • Steel diameter and wall thickness
  • Whether the bollards should be surface-mounted, embedded, core-drilled, removable, or chain-style
  • Concrete condition, slab thickness, slab edges, drainage, and underground utilities
  • Traffic flow, truck paths, forklift routes, pedestrian walkways, and door swing areas
  • Finish requirements such as safety yellow paint, powder coat, galvanized, or owner-selected color
Site photo tip: Send both close-up photos and wide-angle photos. Close-ups show concrete, anchors, slab condition, and mounting surfaces. Wide photos show traffic flow, doors, equipment, utilities, and what the bollards need to protect.

Surface-Mounted vs. Embedded Bollards

One of the first decisions is whether the bollards will be surface-mounted or embedded. Surface-mounted bollards usually use a base plate and anchors into concrete. Embedded bollards may be set into concrete, installed in core-drilled holes, or coordinated with new concrete work.

Surface-mounted bollards can be useful when the owner wants a bolt-down detail or when the bollard location needs to stay above an existing slab. Embedded bollards may be preferred for certain protective layouts, but the site conditions matter.

Concrete thickness, reinforcement, underground utilities, edge distance, drainage, slab condition, and engineered requirements should be confirmed before installation. When bollards are expected to resist vehicle impact or meet a rated security requirement, the system should be specified by the responsible engineer, manufacturer, owner, project documents, or qualified authority.

Are Bollards Crash-Rated?

Standard steel pipe bollards are not automatically crash-rated. A bollard is only crash-rated, anti-ram rated, or vehicle impact-rated when the bollard system, foundation, installation detail, manufacturer requirements, testing, and documentation support that rating.

Many commercial properties use standard protective bollards for general property protection, traffic separation, equipment protection, and visibility. Security-rated applications, public safety requirements, high-risk vehicle impact areas, or engineered protection details should be reviewed and specified by the responsible engineer, owner, manufacturer, project documents, security consultant, or qualified authority.
